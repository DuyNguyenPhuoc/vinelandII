## 1. What the Vineland is

The **Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales (VABS)** is the most widely used standardized measure
of **adaptive behavior** — what a person actually *does* to function day‑to‑day and be
independent — as opposed to what they *can* do on a cognitive/IQ test. It is a
**norm‑referenced, informant‑based** instrument: a trained examiner interviews (or has rated
by) a parent, caregiver, or teacher who knows the individual well; the individual is **not**
tested directly.

- **Publisher:** Pearson (formerly AGS — American Guidance Service).
- **Authors (Vineland‑II):** Sara S. Sparrow, Domenic V. Cicchetti, and David A. Balla.
- **Age range:** birth to 90 years.
- **Primary questions it answers:** How independent is this person in communication, daily
  living, social, and (for young children) motor skills, compared to same‑age peers? Where are
  the strengths and deficits? Is there a developmental delay, and how large?

## 2. History and editions

| Year | Edition | Key facts |
|------|---------|-----------|
| **1935** | **Vineland Social Maturity Scale** | Developed by **Edgar A. Doll** at the *Vineland Training School*, Vineland, New Jersey (which gives the instrument its name). Original categories: self‑help general, self‑help dressing, self‑help eating, self‑direction, socialization, locomotion, occupation. |
| **1984** | **Vineland ABS (1st ed., "VABS")** | Major revision by Sparrow, Balla & Cicchetti, published by AGS. Three domains (Communication, Daily Living Skills, Socialization). Prompted partly by the **Education for All Handicapped Children Act of 1975 (PL 94‑142)**, which required standardized assessment of children with disabilities. |
| **2005** | **Vineland‑II** | Added a fourth domain, **Motor Skills**; expanded forms and norms; the edition translated in this project (AGS © 2005). |
| **2016** | **Vineland‑3** | Current edition (Sparrow, Cicchetti & **Saulnier**). ~34% more items, updated norms and content, digital administration/scoring via Q‑global. |

The Vietnamese *Bản Ghi / Mẫu khảo sát bằng phỏng vấn* in this project is the **Vineland‑II
Survey Interview Form**, described as a revised version of Edgar A. Doll's Vineland Social
Maturity Scale (AGS © 2005).

## 3. Theoretical basis: adaptive behavior

**Adaptive behavior** = the performance of the daily activities required for personal and social
self‑sufficiency. Two defining features:

1. It is about **typical performance**, not maximum ability ("does" not "can").
2. It is **age‑referenced** — expectations rise with age.

The Vineland's domains map onto the three areas of adaptive functioning defined by the
**American Association on Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities (AAIDD)** and by the
**DSM‑5**: conceptual (≈ Communication), social (≈ Socialization), and practical (≈ Daily
Living Skills). This is why the Vineland is explicitly referenced in the **DSM‑5 criteria for
intellectual disability**, where a diagnosis requires deficits in *both* intellectual and
adaptive functioning.

---

## 4. Domain and subdomain structure

Four domains, each split into subdomains (Vietnamese terms as used in the project form):

| Domain (EN) | Domain (VI) | Subdomains (EN) | Subdomains (VI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Communication** | Giao tiếp | Receptive · Expressive · Written | Tiếp nhận · Diễn đạt/Biểu đạt · Văn bản |
| **Daily Living Skills** | Kỹ năng sinh hoạt thường ngày | Personal · Domestic · Community | Cá nhân · Gia đình · Cộng đồng |
| **Socialization** | Xã hội hóa | Interpersonal Relationships · Play & Leisure · Coping Skills | Quan hệ liên cá nhân · Vui chơi & giải trí · Kỹ năng ứng xử |
| **Motor Skills** | Kỹ năng vận động | Gross · Fine | Vận động thô · Vận động tinh |

**Adaptive Behavior Composite (ABC):**
- **Ages birth–6:11** → composite = **all four** domains (Motor Skills included).
- **Ages 7–90** → composite = **three** domains (Communication, Daily Living Skills,
  Socialization); Motor Skills is not part of the composite because motor items become less
  discriminating with age.

Within each subdomain, items are grouped by content clusters and ordered by developmental
difficulty (each item carries the age at which typical children first achieve it — the
`<1→`, `1→`, `2→` markers on the form). This ordering is what makes the **basal/ceiling** rules
work (see §7).

## 5. Maladaptive Behavior domain (optional)

An optional index (ages ≥ 3) that screens for **problem behaviors** that can interfere with
adaptive functioning. Higher scores = more problematic (the direction is reversed from the
adaptive scales). Structure in the project form (*Chỉ báo hành vi kém thích ứng*):

- **Internalizing** (Nội hóa) — e.g., withdrawal, anxiety, sadness, poor eye contact.
- **Externalizing** (Ngoại hóa) — e.g., impulsivity, defiance, aggression, lying/stealing.
- **Other** (Nhóm khác) — e.g., thumb‑sucking, tics, hyperactivity, running away, substance use.
- **Critical Items** (Các mục cốt yếu) — more severe/clinically significant behaviors (e.g.,
  self‑injury, unusual sensory/obsessive behavior, possible psychosis). These are **not summed
  into a score**; each is rated for frequency and severity (T = severe/*trầm trọng*,
  N = mild/*nhẹ*) and interpreted individually.

Maladaptive results are reported on the **v‑scale** (M = 15, SD = 3) with three bands — see §8.

## 6. Forms and administration

Vineland‑II offers four forms:

| Form | Mode | Respondent | Typical time |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Survey Interview Form** | Semi‑structured interview | Parent/caregiver | 20–60 min (+15–30 min to score) |
| **Parent/Caregiver Rating Form** | Questionnaire (self‑rated) | Parent/caregiver | 30–60 min |
| **Expanded Interview Form** | Semi‑structured interview (more items, finer detail, useful for program planning) | Parent/caregiver | 60–90 min |
| **Teacher Rating Form** | Questionnaire | Teacher (classroom‑observable behaviors, incl. academic) | ~20 min |

The Survey Interview and Parent/Caregiver Rating forms contain the **same items**; they differ
only in mode (interviewer‑led vs. self‑completed). The interview uses a
**semi‑structured ("open") technique**: the examiner asks general questions about the person's
activities rather than reading items verbatim, then scores the items from the responses.

**The document in this project is the Vineland‑II Survey Interview Form (Mẫu khảo sát bằng
phỏng vấn).**

## 7. Scoring system

### Item scoring
Each item is circled as:

| Code | Meaning (VI) | Meaning (EN) |
|---|---|---|
| **2** | Thường xuyên | Performs the behavior **usually/habitually and independently** |
| **1** | Đôi khi / phần nào đó | **Sometimes** or partially independently |
| **0** | Không bao giờ | **Never**, or never independently |
| **KB** | Không biết (DK) | Respondent doesn't know |
| **K/P** | Không phù hợp (N/O) | No opportunity / not applicable (only where the item permits it) |

> If a subdomain has **more than 2** "KB/no response" items, that subdomain **cannot be scored**.

### Basal and ceiling (sàn và trần)
To avoid administering every item, each subdomain uses start points by age and basal/ceiling rules:
- **Basal (sàn):** the highest set of **4 consecutive items scored "2"** below the start point;
  all items below are credited as "2". If no basal is established, item 1 is treated as the floor.
- **Ceiling (trần):** the lowest set of **4 consecutive items scored "0"**; testing stops.
  If no ceiling is reached, the last item is the ceiling.

### Score chain
```
Item scores (2/1/0)
   → Subdomain RAW score  (items below basal ×2, plus sum of 2s and 1s in the tested range)
   → Subdomain v-SCALE score        (Table B.1;  M = 15, SD = 3, range 1–24)
   → Domain STANDARD score          (Table B.2;  M = 100, SD = 15)  [sum of the domain's v-scales]
   → Adaptive Behavior COMPOSITE    (sum of domain standard scores → composite table)
```

Each score can also be expressed as:
- **Percentile rank** (Table C.3) — % of same‑age peers scoring at or below.
- **Adaptive level** (Tables C.3/C.4) — descriptive band (see §8).
- **Age equivalent** (Table C.5) — the age at which the person's raw score is typical.
- **90% or 95% confidence interval** (Tables C.1/C.2) — every score is an estimate with error;
  report the band, not just the point value.

## 9. Norms

- **Vineland‑II** was standardized on a **U.S. national sample of ~3,695 individuals**, birth to
  90 years, stratified to match U.S. Census demographics (sex, race/ethnicity, region,
  socioeconomic status).
- Scores are **age‑referenced**: a person is always compared with same‑age peers, so the same
  raw score yields different standard scores at different ages.
- **Caution for other countries:** U.S. norms may not perfectly reflect another population's
  developmental expectations; culturally adapted/renormed versions (e.g., the Vietnamese work in
  §12) exist precisely because of this.

## 10. Psychometric properties

Across editions the Vineland has generally shown strong reliability and validity, and it is the
benchmark adaptive‑behavior measure in both clinical and research use.

- **Reliability:** good **internal consistency**, **test–retest** stability, and
  **inter‑interviewer** agreement across the total population and clinical subgroups.
- **Validity:** strong **construct validity**; correlates with other adaptive measures and
  discriminates clinical from typical groups.
- **Vineland‑II → Vineland‑3 concordance:** correlation coefficients **0.78–0.91** across
  domains/subdomains — strong but not perfect agreement (scores are **not interchangeable**
  across editions; see §13).
- **Known caveat:** because there is no single "gold standard" for adaptive behavior, absolute
  criterion validity is inherently hard to establish.

---

## 11. Clinical uses

- **Diagnosis / differential diagnosis:** intellectual disability (required alongside IQ under
  DSM‑5), autism spectrum disorder (ASD), ADHD, developmental delay, acquired brain injury,
  hearing/visual impairment, dementia.
- **Eligibility** for special education and disability services/benefits.
- **Program & treatment planning:** pinpointing specific skill deficits to target
  (the Expanded form is especially useful here).
- **Progress monitoring / outcome measurement:** widely used as an endpoint in clinical trials
  (e.g., Angelman, Sanfilippo/MPS, Dravet, Phelan‑McDermid syndromes).
- **Research** on development and disability.

**Note on ASD:** low Communication and Socialization scores commonly co‑occur with autism, but
the Vineland measures **adaptive functioning, not autism traits directly** — it supports, but
does not by itself make, a diagnosis.

## 12. The Vietnamese Vineland

### 12a. Published validation study (VVABS)
> **Goldberg, M. R., Dill, C. A., Shin, J. Y., & Nguyen, V. N. (2009).** *Reliability and
> validity of the Vietnamese Vineland Adaptive Behavior Scales with preschool‑age children.*
> **Research in Developmental Disabilities, 30(3), 592–602.** (PubMed 19059758)

- **Version adapted:** the **1984 VABS** was translated and culturally adapted into a Vietnamese
  version (**VVABS**).
- **Cultural adaptation:** **17 items** were modified for semantic/contextual relevance to
  Vietnamese culture.
- **Sample:** **120 Vietnamese mothers** of typically developing preschool‑age children in
  kindergarten programs (plus a comparison involving children with intellectual disabilities).
- **Findings:** acceptable **internal‑consistency reliability** and **construct validity**; the
  VVABS **successfully discriminated** Vietnamese children with intellectual disabilities from
  typically developing peers.
- **Conclusion:** a successful adaptation of the adaptive‑behavior construct to a non‑Western
  culture.

### 12b. The edition in this project
The project's form is a **later Vietnamese translation of the Vineland‑II (2005) Survey
Interview Form** — a different, fuller instrument than the 1984‑based VVABS research adaptation
above. Practical implication: unless a Vietnamese renorming exists, results are typically
scored against **U.S. Vineland‑II norms**, and age‑equivalents / delay estimates are the most
defensible outputs to emphasize when norm‑appropriateness is uncertain.

## 13. Vineland‑3 (what changed)

- **Same domain/subdomain structure** as Vineland‑II.
- **~34% more items**, updated content to reflect modern daily life, and **new norms**.
- **Digital administration & scoring** (Q‑global) plus paper.
- **Scores run lower than Vineland‑II**, especially at lower ability levels: in concordance
  research, **~77% of participants had a lower Adaptive Behavior Composite on Vineland‑3** than
  on Vineland‑II. → **Do not compare Vineland‑II and Vineland‑3 scores directly** when tracking a
  person over time; a "drop" may be an edition artifact.

---

## 14. Limitations and cautions

- **Informant‑dependent:** accuracy hinges on how well the respondent knows the person and how
  candid/accurate they are (the form even records the interviewer's judgment of informant
  reliability). Different informants (parent vs. teacher) can yield different pictures.
- **"Does," not "can":** underperformance from lack of opportunity is scored the same as
  inability — hence the K/P option and clinical judgment.
- **Norm fit:** U.S. norms may not fit other populations; edition and language matter.
- **Screening vs. diagnosis:** contributes to, but does not alone establish, any diagnosis.
- **Scoring integrity:** basal/ceiling and the ">2 KB → don't score" rule must be applied
  correctly; a single mis‑scored pivotal subdomain can shift interpretation.

## 15. The scored case in this project

From `Phiếu tóm tắt điểm Vineland II.docx` — child **Nguyen Minh Bảo**, DOB 19 Sep 2017,
**chronological age 4y 6m** at assessment.

**Domain standard scores (M = 100, SD = 15):**

| Domain | Std score | %ile | Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Communication (Giao tiếp) | 67 | 1% | Low |
| Daily Living Skills | 66 | 1% | Low |
| Socialization (Xã hội hóa) | 65 | 1% | Low |
| Motor Skills (Vận động) | 70 | 2% | Low |

Sum of domain standard scores = **268** → estimated **Adaptive Behavior Composite ≈ 63–66**
(~**1st percentile**, roughly 2–2.3 SD below the mean). A **globally low, flat profile**.

**Subdomain age‑equivalents vs. a 54‑month chronological age:**

| Subdomain | Age‑equiv | Approx. delay | v‑scale | Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Coping (ứng xử) | 0:7 | ~47 mo | 8 | Low |
| Interpersonal (quan hệ xã hội) | 0:8 | ~46 mo | 8 | Low |
| Expressive (diễn đạt) | 1:2 | ~40 mo | 5 | Low |
| Receptive (tiếp nhận) | 1:9 | ~33 mo | 9 | Low |
| Play & Leisure | 1:11 | ~31 mo | 10 | Mod. Low |
| Domestic (gia đình) | 2:2 | ~28 mo | 11 | Mod. Low |
| Personal (cá nhân) | 2:4 | ~26 mo | 8 | Low |
| Gross Motor (vận động thô) | 2:4 | ~26 mo | 10 | Mod. Low |
| Community (cộng đồng) | 2:6 | ~24 mo | 10 | Mod. Low |
| Fine Motor (vận động tinh) | 2:8 | ~22 mo | 10 | Mod. Low |
| **Written (văn bản)** | **4:6** | **~0 (age‑level)** | **15** | **Adequate** |

**Interpretation highlights**
1. **Significant global adaptive delay** — most skills at a 1–2.5‑year level in a 4½‑year‑old.
2. **Social/emotional is the floor** — Interpersonal and Coping at a **7–8 month** level
   (~4‑year delay), the deepest deficits.
3. **Expressive ≪ Receptive** (v = 5 vs 9) — understands far more than expressed.
4. **One island of strength: Written** (v = 15, age‑appropriate) amid ~1‑year spoken language —
   a hyperlexia‑like splinter skill.

**Pattern:** flat, ~1st‑percentile composite with social‑communication + coping weakest and a
preserved rote/academic (written) skill — the profile most often associated with **ASD** on the
Vineland, though on its own also consistent with global developmental delay. The Vineland
supports, not replaces, formal diagnosis.

**Caveats specific to this sheet:** confidence intervals and the ABC total are left blank; the
Written result (raw 10 → v 15) is the pivot of the interpretation and is worth double‑checking
against the norms tables; the Maladaptive Behavior section was not scored.
